## 1. Goal and scope

- **Goal:** no supplier is paid late without a reason. The owner sees every unpaid invoice with its due date, records payments with the paid date, gets in-app reminders before and after the due date, gets a weekly in-app summary by supplier, and can open a statement per supplier for the monthly settlement.
- **In scope:**
  - Payable status transitions (`open`, `scheduled`, `paid` with a required paid date, `disputed`) through one definer procedure, `public.set_payable_status`, with an `audit_event` per change. A paid payable can only be reopened as a **reversal with a reason** (AT5).
  - Read functions `public.payable_list`, `public.payable_detail` and `public.supplier_statement`. The statement balance is Σ open invoices − Σ credit memos (AT4).
  - Four cost-bearing, owner-only alert kinds: `payable_due_7d`, `payable_due_today`, `payable_overdue_3d` and `payables_weekly_digest`.
  - Two hourly rules called from `app.alert_rules_body`, one savepoint per tenant × rule, raising through `app.try_raise_alert`.
  - API: `GET /api/payables`, `GET /api/payables/:id`, `PATCH /api/payables/:id`, `GET /api/suppliers/:id/statement`.
  - Screens: `/pagamenti`, `/pagamenti/[id]`, `/anagrafiche/fornitori/[id]/estratto-conto`, plus a nav tab, a home link and a link from payable alerts on `/avvisi`.
  - Race cases, a pgTAP guard that M8 survives the merge, an integration suite, and Playwright at 360 px.
- **Out of scope:**
  - The accountant export (M36).
  - Any outbound channel: email, Telegram, push.
  - Editing a due date or an amount. Both stay as approval froze them, and a wrong invoice is disputed.
  - Undoing an approval (M5 Q8 → M5 V8).
  - Separate credit-note documents (note di credito), which are variant V2.
  - A "scheduled for" date.
  - Payment methods and bank data. None are stored.

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Decisions

1. **Status stays a column on `payable`; the history is `audit_event`; no new status-history table.**
   - **The invariants:**
     - "Balances are derived, never stored" is about stock and portion quantities (plan §3.6 rule 1). A status is a state, not a balance.
     - "Ledgers are append-only" is met by `audit_event`, which is append-only for every role and records `before`, `after`, `reason`, who and when for every change. Plan §3.5.1 already says payable state changes go "only through a procedure and recorded in audit_event".
   - **What makes the column safe:**
     - no API role has UPDATE on `payable`;
     - the guard is tightened so that `status`, `paid_on` and `paid_at` can change **only** inside `set_payable_status`, through a transaction-local flag the procedure sets and clears around its one UPDATE. Even the migration owner gets `payable_immutable`;
     - every change the procedure makes writes its `audit_event` in the same transaction;
     - `due_date` becomes immutable (no M9 path changes it).
   - **Why not an append-only `payable_status_event` table with the status derived from it:** every reader (list, statement, reminders, digest) would need a "latest event" join. It would duplicate `audit_event` and buy no extra guarantee. Rejected in §4 and ADR-009.
2. **The paid date is a calendar date: new column `paid_on date`.**
   - Check `payable_paid_on_ck`: `(status = 'paid') = (paid_on is not null)`.
   - `paid_at` keeps its M5 check and now means "when the payment was recorded" (`clock_timestamp()`). It is set and cleared together with `paid_on`.
   - Rules:
     - `paid_on` is required for `paid` (`paid_on_required`) and refused for any other status (`paid_on_not_allowed`);
     - it may not be after today's venue date (`paid_on_future`);
     - there is no lower bound (payment on delivery can precede the invoice date).
3. **Transition matrix (one SQL function, `app.payable_allowed_targets(status)`, read by the procedure and returned by `payable_detail`, so the UI never computes it):**

   | From \ to | open | scheduled | paid | disputed |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | open | no-op | ✓ | ✓ (paid date) | ✓ |
   | scheduled | ✓ | no-op | ✓ (paid date) | ✓ |
   | disputed | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ (paid date) | no-op |
   | paid | **reversal only** (reason required) | 409 | no-op if same date, else 409 | 409 |

   - **No-op:** same status (and, for paid, the same paid date) returns `changed = false` and writes no audit row, so a double tap is harmless.
   - **Leaving paid without a reversal:** 409 `payable_paid_locked`.
   - **Reversal (AT5):** `p_reversal = true` with a non-empty reason, from `paid` to `open` only. It clears `paid_on` and `paid_at` and writes the audit action `payable.payment_reversed` with the reason.
     - A reversal of a payable that is not paid → 409 `payable_not_paid`.
     - A reversal to any status other than `open` → 422 `reversal_target_invalid`.
     - A missing reason → 422 `reason_required`.
   - A reason (1–200 characters) is optional on other transitions and stored in `audit_event.reason`. The form hint says "Non inserire dati personali.", and the reason is never logged.
4. **Roles (plan §3.11.2):**
   - payables: owner and admin read and write;
   - chef, waiter, or no claims → 42501 (API 403);
   - payable **alerts** are owner only (M6 decision);
   - the procedure checks the role first, uses `search_path = ''`, takes the tenant from `app.tenant_id()`, and is executable by `authenticated` only (not `anon`, not `service_role`);
   - the read functions follow the same pattern (definer, role check, explicit tenant filter).
5. **Reminders: one stage per run, catch-up, deduplicated on the stage's nominal date.**
   - **Today** = `app.alert_business_date(tenant, p_at)` (Europe/Rome calendar day, DST by the time zone database). `p_at` is the fake clock in pgTAP.
   - **Stage** for an `open` payable with due date `D` = the latest stage reached:
     - `payable_overdue_3d` if today ≥ D+3;
     - else `payable_due_today` if today ≥ D;
     - else `payable_due_7d` if today ≥ D−7;
     - else none.
   - The alert is raised with `p_business_date` = the stage's nominal date (D−7, D or D+3), entity `payable`/`id`. The M6 dedup key therefore fires **each stage once per payable**, whatever day it is first seen.
   - **Why catch-up rather than "exactly on the day":**
     - if the hourly job fails for a whole day, an exact-day rule loses that reminder for good;
     - a payable approved after D−7 (for example due in 3 days) would never get its first reminder.
   - With catch-up, AT1 still holds (12-03, 12-10, 12-13), and a payable first seen on 12-11 gets only `payable_due_today`, never a stale 7-day reminder.
   - **Eligibility:** status `open` only (Q29 default; V1 adds `scheduled` for the +3 d stage). `scheduled`, `paid` and `disputed` get nothing.
   - **Fully credited payables are excluded:** a payable whose `amount` is 0,00 EUR (gross = credited) raises no reminder (`p.amount > 0`). It stays open and listed in the payables screens.
   - The rule reads eligible payables with `for share skip locked`. A payable whose status is being changed right now is skipped and caught up next hour (race case `pay-vs-rules`).
   - **Before raising,** the rule checks whether the alert for (kind, payable, nominal date) already exists. If it does, it skips the call and builds no payload. So the hourly re-runs cost one indexed lookup per payable, and the rule's count is the number of alerts it raised. In a concurrent second run, `raise_alert`'s `on conflict do nothing` still keeps one row, and the count may then over-report by one; it is informational only.
6. **Weekly digest: one in-app alert per tenant per ISO week.**
   - Kind `payables_weekly_digest`, entity `tenant`/tenant id, `p_business_date` = the ISO Monday of today's venue date (`date_trunc('week', today)`, which is ISO in Postgres). It fires on the first hourly run of the week, normally Monday 00:xx Europe/Rome, and catches up later in the week if runs failed.
   - Content: every unpaid payable (`open`, `scheduled`, `disputed`; Q32), grouped by supplier.
   - No unpaid payables → no digest that week.
   - **Fully credited (0,00 EUR) payables are excluded** from the digest: from the totals and from the supplier list (`p.amount > 0` in both queries). A supplier whose only unpaid payables are 0,00 EUR is not listed, and a tenant with nothing else unpaid gets no digest.
   - The rule checks for an existing digest row first, so the payload is built at most once per week.
   - The digest is a snapshot at its creation, and its body says so ("al 26/10/2026"). The live list is `/pagamenti`.
7. **Rule isolation (lead guardrail 3; M7 §8 (b), adapted).**
   - `app.alert_rules_body(p_at)` loops over tenants in id order and calls each rule in its own `begin … exception when others` block (one savepoint per tenant × rule).
   - A failure rolls back only that tenant's run of that rule. It is recorded with M8's `app.record_alert_refusal(tenant, null, 'tenant', tenant, '<rule>', 'rule_failed', sqlstate)`, which writes ids and codes only plus a warning. The other rules and tenants commit.
   - Inside a rule, every alert goes through `app.try_raise_alert(..., p_caller => '<rule>')`. A guard refusal of one payable is absorbed (one `app.alert_refusal` row) and the loop continues.
   - Item counts are added only after a block returns, so a rolled-back block adds 0.
   - **Deviation from M7 §8:** M7 proposed replacing `app.run_alert_rules` once, to record `rule_partial` in `job_run`. The lead's guardrail forbids replacing the runner, so `job_run` stays `ok = true` when only some tenant × rule blocks failed, and those failures live in `app.alert_refusal` (code `rule_failed`). **Note for M13:** its job-health check must read `app.alert_refusal` rows with code `rule_failed`, not only `job_run`.
   - Future rule milestones (M10, M16) `create or replace app.alert_rules_body` starting from M9's version. Their pgTAP must re-run the key cases of M9's `073` and `074` to prove the payable rules still run.
8. **Statement = unpaid payables of one supplier and their credit memos.**
   - Unpaid = `open`, `scheduled`, `disputed`.
   - Totals:
     - `invoicesTotal` = Σ `gross_amount`;
     - `creditsTotal` = Σ `credited_amount`;
     - `balance` = `invoicesTotal − creditsTotal`, which equals Σ `amount` (the M5 check constraint makes this exact);
     - `disputedAmount` = the share of `balance` under dispute (Q31 default: included in the balance and shown separately).
   - Credits come from the frozen `payable.credited_amount`. They are never recomputed from lines, so the statement and the payable cannot disagree.
   - Paid payables are not on the statement.
   - "As of" = today's venue date.
9. **No new public table, no new dependency, no new secret, no new cron job or route.** The existing hourly `alert-rules` job runs the new rules.

### 3.2 Data model and procedures (migrations from `20261024100000`; never `20261023110000`–`110800`, which are M8's)

**`20261024100000_payable_status.sql`** (step 1, before the merge)
- `alter table public.payable add column paid_on date`, plus `payable_paid_on_ck check ((status = 'paid') = (paid_on is not null))`. Existing rows are all `open`, so the check holds.
- `create or replace function app.payable_guard()` (M5's body, unchanged except as follows; M8 does not replace it):
  - also blocks `due_date`;
  - allows a change of `status`, `paid_on` or `paid_at` only when `current_setting('app.payable_transition', true) = 'on'`, else P0001 `payable_immutable`.
- **Audit lists** (one migration each, as M7 and M8 did):
  - entity `('invoice','ingredient','recipe','tenant','payable')`;
  - action = M7's list + M8's `'tenant.settings_changed'` + `'payable.status_changed'`, `'payable.payment_reversed'`.
  - M8's values are included on purpose: this migration then runs correctly both before the merge (no M8 rows exist) and after it (in timestamp order it runs after M8's `20261023110000`, so dropping and re-adding the check keeps every M8 value valid).
- `app.require_payables_writer()` and `app.require_payables_reader()`: owner or admin, else 42501. No API role can execute them.
- `app.payable_allowed_targets(p_status public.payable_status) returns public.payable_status[]` (immutable): the matrix of §3.1.3 without reversals.
- `public.set_payable_status(p_payable uuid, p_status public.payable_status, p_paid_on date default null, p_reason text default null, p_reversal boolean default false) returns jsonb` (definer, `search_path = ''`). Order:
  1. `perform app.require_payables_writer()`.
  2. Validate the reason: trimmed, at most 200 characters, else P0001 `invalid_request`.
  3. `select … for no key update` the payable where `tenant_id = app.tenant_id()`. Not found → P0002 `not_found`.
  4. Paid-date rules (§3.1.2):
     - `paid_on_required` / `paid_on_not_allowed`;
     - `paid_on_future` when `p_paid_on > app.venue_date(now(), tenant.timezone)`.
  5. Reversal path: not paid → `payable_not_paid`; target not `open` → `reversal_target_invalid`; no reason → `reason_required`.
  6. Normal path:
     - same status (and the same `paid_on` when paid) → return `{changed: false}`;
     - from `paid` → `payable_paid_locked`;
     - target not in `app.payable_allowed_targets(old)` → `payable_transition_invalid` (unreachable with today's matrix; kept as the general guard).
  7. `set_config('app.payable_transition','on',true)` → UPDATE `status`, `paid_on`, `paid_at` (`clock_timestamp()` or null) → `set_config('app.payable_transition','',true)`.
  8. Insert `audit_event` (`payable`, id, `payable.status_changed` or `payable.payment_reversed`, `before {status, paid_on}`, `after {status, paid_on}`, `reason`, `created_by = app.app_user_id()`).
  9. Return `{payableId, status, paidOn, changed: true}`.
- Execute: revoke from `public, anon, service_role`; grant to `authenticated`.

**`20261024100100_payable_reads.sql`** (step 2, before the merge). All three functions are definer, call `app.require_payables_reader()` first, filter `tenant_id = app.tenant_id()`, compute today with `app.venue_date(now(), tenant.timezone)`, and are executable by `authenticated` only.
- `public.payable_list(p_filter text default 'unpaid', p_supplier uuid default null, p_cursor_date date default null, p_cursor_id uuid default null, p_limit int default 50) returns jsonb`:
  - **Filters:**
    - `unpaid` (open, scheduled, disputed): ordered `due_date asc, id asc`;
    - `paid`: ordered `paid_on desc, id desc`;
    - `all`: ordered `due_date desc, id desc`;
    - any other filter → P0001 `invalid_request`.
  - The function reads `p_limit + 1` rows to know whether a next page exists.
  - **Returns** `{items: [PayableItem], nextCursor: {date, id} | null, totals: {count, amount} (unpaid filter only, over all unpaid rows of the tenant or supplier, not just the page), today}`.
- `public.payable_detail(p_payable uuid) returns jsonb`: `{payable: PayableItem + {grossAmount, creditedAmount, dueDateSource, paidAt}, allowedStatuses: app.payable_allowed_targets(status) (empty when paid), canReversePayment: status = 'paid', history: [{action, fromStatus, toStatus, paidOn, reason, at, byName}]}`.
  - The history is the payable's `audit_event` rows, oldest first, preceded by one `created` entry (`payable.created_at`).
  - `byName` comes from `app_user.display_name`.
  - Not found or another tenant → P0002.
- `public.supplier_statement(p_supplier uuid) returns jsonb`: `{supplier: {id, name, vatNumber}, asOf: today, invoices: [PayableItem] (unpaid, due_date asc), credits: [{payableId, invoiceId, invoiceNumber, invoiceDate, creditedAmount}] (unpaid payables with credited_amount > 0), totals: {invoicesTotal, creditsTotal, balance, disputedAmount}}`.
  - A supplier of another tenant, or unknown → P0002.
  - A supplier with no unpaid payable → empty lists and zero totals.
- `PayableItem = {id, invoiceId, invoiceNumber (number_raw), invoiceDate, supplierId, supplierName, amount, dueDate, status, paidOn, daysToDue (due_date − today), overdue (unpaid and due_date < today)}`.
  - Amounts are JSON numbers with 2 decimals, formatted only in TS.

  - loop over `payable` where `tenant_id = p_tenant and status = 'open' and amount > 0 and due_date <= today + 7`, ordered by `due_date, id`, `for share skip locked`;
  - compute the stage and its nominal date (§3.1.5);
  - if an alert with (tenant, kind, `'payable'`, id, nominal date) exists, skip it;
  - else `v_id := app.try_raise_alert(p_tenant, <kind>, 'payable', id, payload, null, null, p_at, <nominal date>, 'payable_reminders')`, adding 1 when `v_id` is not null;
  - **Payload** (cost-bearing, owner only): `{payable_id, invoice_id, invoice_number (≤80), supplier_id, supplier_name (≤80), amount_eur, due_date}`.
- `app.payables_digest_payload(p_tenant uuid, p_today date) returns jsonb`:
  - `{week_start_date, as_of_date, payables_count, amount_eur, overdue_count, overdue_amount_eur, suppliers_count, suppliers_listed, suppliers: [{supplier_id, supplier_name (≤80), payables_count, amount_eur, overdue_count, next_due_date}]}`;

- `app.payables_digest_rule(p_tenant uuid, p_at timestamptz) returns integer`:
  - Monday = `date_trunc('week', today)::date`;
  - if a `payables_weekly_digest` alert for (tenant, Monday) exists → 0;
  - else build the payload; null → 0;
  - else `try_raise_alert(p_tenant, 'payables_weekly_digest', 'tenant', p_tenant, payload, null, null, p_at, monday, 'payables_digest')`, returning 1 if raised.

  - return `v_items`.
- Revoke all on the four new functions from `public, anon, authenticated, service_role`.

## 4. Alternatives considered

- **Append-only `payable_status_event` table, with status derived from the latest event:** rejected (§3.1.1). `audit_event` is already the append-only history. A derived status would add a "latest event" join to four readers and to the hourly rule, and no invariant asks for it (status is not a quantity balance). The guard flag closes the "status changed without an audit row" gap that the table would otherwise address.
- **Exact-day reminders (fire only on D−7, D, D+3):** rejected (§3.1.5). One failed day of the hourly job, or an approval after D−7, loses reminders for good. Catch-up with nominal-date dedup fires each stage once and never late-fires a stale earlier stage.
- **Reminders deduplicated on the raise day (the M6 default):** rejected. During a catch-up window it would fire the same stage every day.
- **Replacing `app.run_alert_rules` to record `rule_partial` (M7 §8 (b) as written):** rejected by the lead's guardrail (the M6 contract). Partial failures are recorded in M8's `app.alert_refusal` (`rule_failed`); M13 reads them.
- **M9's own isolated-raise helper and failure table, independent of M8:** rejected once the lead fixed the merge order (M8 first). It would duplicate `try_raise_alert` and `alert_refusal`.
- **One savepoint per payable instead of per tenant × rule:** not needed. Guard refusals are already absorbed per alert by `try_raise_alert`, and any other error is a bug that should stop that tenant's rule for the hour, not be skipped silently.
- **Statement recomputing credits from `invoice_line.credit_qty`:** rejected. It would duplicate M5's credit formula (VAT included, Q5/V5) and could disagree with the frozen payable. `payable.credited_amount` is the single source.
- **Digest as a materialised weekly report table:** rejected. The M6 alert row already stores a structured payload, deduplicates per week and has acknowledgement.
- **Email digest through Resend:** rejected by the founder (2026-10-23).
- **Computing allowed actions, day counts or totals in TS or React:** rejected (invariant: no data logic in components). SQL returns them.

## 6. Risks and mitigations

- **Security / privacy**
  - *Definer procedures bypass RLS:* role check first, `search_path = ''`, tenant from `app.tenant_id()`, execute for `authenticated` only. The rule functions have no API role (steps 1, 2, 11).
  - *Status changed without an audit row:* the guard refuses status writes outside `set_payable_status`'s flag window, for every role (step 1).
  - *€ amounts reaching the chef or a waiter:*
    - payables, statement and audit are owner/admin by RLS and procedure checks;
    - the four kinds are cost-bearing, so the registry, the table check and `raise_alert` refuse a chef audience;
    - admin reads no alerts (M6);
    - proved in steps 1, 2, 10 and 11.
  - *Personal data:*
    - payloads and screens name suppliers, who may be sole traders (project Q13);
    - the free-text reason is capped at 200 characters, carries the hint "Non inserire dati personali.", is never logged, and is stored only in `audit_event`;
    - no bank data or payment method is stored;
    - nothing leaves Supabase (EU), and there is no provider;
    - error reports carry SQLSTATEs and ids only (step 4);
    - retention: payables and audit follow the 10-year invoice retention (plan §3.11.8); alerts follow M6 Q3 (kept; M6 V2 if a period is chosen).
  - *IDOR:* malformed or foreign ids → 404, via P0002 from the tenant-filtered lookups (steps 2, 4, 7).
  - *CSRF:* PATCH requires `application/json` (`withApi`).
- **Correctness**
  - *Alerts only in the app:* a reminder is seen only when the owner opens `/avvisi`. This is the accepted consequence of the founder's decision (ADR-006). The digest and the `/pagamenti` overdue badges reduce the risk.
  - *The digest is a snapshot:* it is created on Monday and stays as it was. Its text says "al …", and `/pagamenti` is live.
  - *A partial failure looks green in `job_run`:* per-tenant × rule failures are in `app.alert_refusal` (`rule_failed`), and M13 must read them (§3.1.7).
  - *A later milestone replacing `alert_rules_body` drops M9's rules:* M10 and M16 start from M9's body, and their pgTAP re-runs the key cases of `073` and `074` (§3.1.7).
  - *Approval reversal (M5 Q8 → V8), if built later:* it must change the payable through a new procedure (and a new status such as `cancelled`). Rules select only `open`, so a cancelled payable would get no reminder. Out of M9.
  - *Due-date fallback (M5 Q6 → V6):* a payable whose due date is its invoice date gets `payable_due_today` on its first hourly run after approval (catch-up), as intended.
  - *Merge with M8:*
    - mitigated by the order (M8 first) and the superset audit list (step 1);
    - step 9 proves M8 survives (`076`), with a grep that M9 replaces none of M8's functions;
    - M9's migrations (`202610241…`) sort after M8's (`2026102311…`), so `supabase db push` applies them in order.
- **Unverified (checked in the named step):**
  - **T1** `date_trunc('week', d)` gives the ISO Monday, including across the year end: `074` (step 11).
  - **T2** `for share skip locked` in a plpgsql loop skips a row held by `for no key update` and does not wait: race `pay-vs-rules` (step 12).
- **Performance / cost:** tens of payables per tenant; one indexed scan per tenant per hour, plus one dedup lookup per eligible payable. No provider and no running cost from M9. No performance figure is promised.
- **Dependencies:** none added.
- **Plan changes to record in DigiAlto-Ops `context.md`:**
  - M9 AT3 is an in-app digest (deviation above);
  - `payable.paid_on` is added (`paid_at` = recording time);
  - reminders deduplicate on the stage's nominal date and catch up;
  - `alert_rules_body` isolates per tenant × rule, and failures go to `app.alert_refusal` (M7 §8's `rule_partial` dropped by the lead's guardrail; M13 must read `alert_refusal`);
  - ADR-009 is added.

## 7. Open questions for the founder (defaults in force for this build)

**Decided (founder, 2026-10-23, via M6):** alerts are in the app only, and the owner is the only recipient. The weekly digest is therefore an in-app alert, and payable reminders are for the owner only.

- **Q29 Does a "scheduled" (programmato) payment still get the reminder 3 days after the due date?**
  - **Default in force:** no. Reminders (7 days before, on the day, 3 days after) only while the payable is "Da pagare" (`open`). Scheduled and disputed payables get none.
  - **Alternative:** scheduled payments also get the reminder 3 days after the due date, in case the transfer did not go through (→ V1).
- **Q30 Do your suppliers send separate credit notes (note di credito), or are returns always written on the invoice?**
  - **Default in force:** the statement's credits are the short or damaged quantities you record on the invoice lines before approval (M4/M5).
  - **Alternative:** record separate credit notes per supplier, which lower the statement balance (→ V2).
- **Q31 Should disputed invoices count in the supplier balance?**
  - **Default in force:** yes, with the disputed share shown on its own line ("di cui contestate").
  - **Alternative:** excluded from the balance and listed apart (→ V3).
- **Q32 Which payables should the weekly summary list?**
  - **Default in force:** everything not yet paid (to pay, scheduled and disputed), grouped by supplier, with what is overdue.
  - **Alternative:** only those still to pay (→ V4).
- **Assumptions (confirm; a different answer means a re-plan, not a variant):**
  - **A1:** owner and admin (DigiAlto support) can record payments (role matrix §3.11.2); only the owner receives the reminders.
  - **A2:** a paid date cannot be in the future. A planned payment is marked "programmato" and has no date.
  - **A3:** due dates and amounts are not editable after approval. A wrong invoice is marked "contestata".
  - **A4:** the weekly summary appears on Monday at the first hourly run after midnight (Italian time) and is not updated during the week.
  - **A5:** undoing a payment always returns the invoice to "Da pagare", with a reason.
- **Dependencies on M5 questions still open:**
  - M5 Q6 (due-date fallback → V6): reminders then fire right after approval;
  - M5 Q8 (undo approval → V8): would need a cancelled payable state, outside M9 (§6);
  - M5 Q5 (credits with or without VAT → V5): changes the credited amounts the statement shows, not M9's logic.
