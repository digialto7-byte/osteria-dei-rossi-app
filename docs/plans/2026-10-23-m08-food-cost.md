## 1. Goal and scope

- **Goal:** the owner sees the food cost, fixed cost, full cost, margins, GP% and status of every plate, computed from approved invoices and the recipe versions, correct as soon as an approval commits and correct for any past date; the chef sees the same plates as percentages only; an approval that moves an ingredient price beyond the threshold raises one owner alert naming every affected recipe and its new margin; and the admin can compare the app's plate costs with the chef's spreadsheet for Gate 1.
- **In scope:**
  - Ingredient cost basis: moving average of the last N purchases (N = tenant setting, default 3) or last price, per ingredient, with last, max, average and purchase count visible; the per-ingredient basis as an append-only history table `ingredient_cost_basis`, changed by owner/admin.

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Decisions

1. **Costs are derived on read, at an explicit instant (ADR-008).** One engine, `app.recipe_costs(p_tenant, p_keys, p_at)`, computes from committed rows; nothing is stored or refreshed. An approval is therefore visible in the next read after its commit (AT4 is met by construction, and proved with a timer, step 15). `p_at = null` means "now": current versions, every price row, the current basis. A non-null `p_at` means "in force at that instant":
   - recipe version: the highest `version` of the key with `valid_from ≤ p_at`;
   - prices: rows with `effective_on ≤ app.venue_date(p_at, tenant.timezone)` (the invoice date, M5 Q1);
   - basis: the newest `ingredient_cost_basis` row with `valid_from ≤ p_at`, else `moving_average`.
   - For a calendar date `D` the instant is the end of that Rome day, `app.venue_day_end(D, tz)` = start of `D + 1` in Europe/Rome minus 1 µs (DST-safe: the 25-hour day 2026-10-25 ends at 22:59:59.999999Z).
2. **What a purchase is and what its price means.** A purchase is **one `price_history` row** = one received stock invoice line (M5). Its `unit_price` is € per ingredient base unit (`kg`, `l`, `pz`), **net of VAT**, the price charged (`line_amount ÷ (qty × f)`). Consequences:
   - a **partial credit** (M5 `credit_qty`) does not change the unit price: it reduces the quantity received and the payable, not the price per unit;
   - a **fully credited line** has no price row, so it is not a purchase;
   - the **VAT of a credit** (M5 Q5, credit includes its VAT) affects only the payable; prices are net;
   - two lines of the same ingredient on one invoice are two purchases (A1);
   - split children (M4) are purchases like any line; the supplier does not matter (the basis is per ingredient across suppliers). `supplier_item.last_price` stays as M5 writes it (per supplier item, for M16 purchase suggestions); M8 reads `price_history` only (M5 §4 asked M8 to revisit this: decided).
   - Order of purchases: `effective_on desc, seq desc` (the M5 `last_price` order).
3. **Cost basis per ingredient (PRD 1.4, AT3):**
   - `avg` = arithmetic mean of the `unit_price` of the last N purchases in force (N = `avg_cost_n`, default 3; fewer if fewer exist), not weighted by quantity (Q1);
   - `last` = the newest purchase's price; `max` = the highest price of all purchases in force (Q2); `purchases_used` = how many rows went into `avg`; `last_purchase_on`;
   - `unit_cost` = `avg` when the basis is `moving_average`, `last` when `last_price`;
   - **no purchase in force → `unit_cost` null.** Never zero.
   - The basis setting is an **append-only history** `ingredient_cost_basis` (not a column on `ingredient`, which the chef can read): owner and admin append a row through `set_ingredient_cost_basis`; the newest row in force wins; no row = `moving_average` (Q11).
4. **Recursive cost (PRD 1.4, AT1, AT2, AT7).** Per line, in the version in force:
   - unit factor `f(unit)`: `kg, l, pz, porz` → 1; `g, ml` → 0.001 (`app.recipe_unit_factor`, the same factors as M5's `app.base_factor` and M7's units);
   - **ingredient line:** `qty × f × unit_cost ÷ yield_factor` (qty is the net usable quantity, M7; 1 kg at 0.82 → `unit_cost ÷ 0.82` per usable kg, AT1);
   - **sub line:** `qty × f × (sub_total ÷ sub.output_qty) ÷ yield_factor`; the line's unit must have the dimension of the sub version's `output_unit`, else the line is `unit_mismatch` (incomplete; M7 validates against the *current* sub version, a past version may differ);
   - **batch line (`porz`, plates only):** `qty × (batch_total ÷ portions_per_batch) ÷ yield_factor`;
   - **recipe total** = Σ lines. **Unit cost:** batch → per portion (`total ÷ portions_per_batch`), sub → per output base unit (`total ÷ output_qty`), plate → per plate (= total, the food cost).
   - Sub-recipes and batches resolve to **their** version in force at the same instant (keys, M7 decision 2).
   - **Incomplete propagation:** a line is incomplete when its ingredient has no cost, its sub/batch is incomplete, its sub/batch has no version in force (`sub_not_in_force`), or its unit cannot convert (`unit_mismatch`). A recipe with any incomplete line is incomplete: `total_cost` and `unit_cost` null, `reasons` (distinct codes) and `missing_ingredient_ids` (the union from below) set. Never a partial sum presented as the cost.
   - Precision: `numeric` without rounding inside; outputs rounded half away from zero to 4 decimals for € and 2 decimals for percentages (plan §3.5 money convention); the screens format to cents.
5. **Graph walk: distinct nodes, then levels (M7 pattern).**
   1. Collect the reachable recipes from the roots with a recursive CTE whose rows are `(key, depth)` joined with `union` (each recipe once per depth, never once per path), resolving each key's version at `p_at`; depth ≤ 13 (one beyond the cap, as `recipe_allergen_summary`).
   2. One call of `app.ingredient_unit_costs` for every ingredient reached.
   3. Compute bottom-up in rounds: each round is one set-based query that costs every not-yet-costed recipe whose sub/batch lines all point at costed recipes (Kahn's order), storing results in a plpgsql `jsonb` map keyed by recipe key. At most 13 rounds.
   4. Any recipe left after 13 rounds (only possible with data the M7 guard refuses: a hand-written loop or a chain over 12) is incomplete with reason `graph_too_deep` (fail closed, as `recipe_allergen_summary`).
   The work is O(recipes × lines), so the 4 × 10 diamond of `054` stays fast (performance test `062`, step 9).
6. **Plate outputs (PRD 1.4, AT6, AT8).** For each plate at the instant:
   - `food_cost` = the plate's recipe cost (null if incomplete);
   - `sale_price` = `recipe_price.sale_price` of the version in force (VAT included, M7 Q3); `vat_pct` = tenant `sale_vat_pct` (default 10, Q3); `sale_price_ex_vat` = `sale_price ÷ (1 + vat_pct ÷ 100)`;
   - `fixed_cost` = the per-plate fixed cost of §3.1.7 (null when not allocated);
   - `full_cost` = `food_cost + fixed_cost` (null if either is null);
   - `gross_margin` = `sale_price_ex_vat − food_cost`; `net_margin` = `sale_price_ex_vat − full_cost`;
   - `food_cost_pct` = `food_cost ÷ sale_price_ex_vat × 100`; `gp_pct` = `gross_margin ÷ sale_price_ex_vat × 100` (GP on food cost, Q4); `target_gp_pct` from `recipe_price`;
   - **status**, first that applies: `incomplete` (food cost unknown, or no sale price, or a sale price of 0) → `below_target` (`gp_pct < target_gp_pct`) → `fixed_cost_unallocated` (fixed cost null) → `ok`. Below-target outranks unallocated on purpose: until M19 every plate is unallocated, and a margin problem must not be hidden by it. A separate boolean `fixed_cost_allocated` is always returned to the owner, so the owner screen shows both. **Admin (founder 2026-10-23: fixed costs owner only):** `fixed_cost`, `full_cost`, `net_margin` and `fixed_cost_allocated` are null, and the status is computed without `fixed_cost_unallocated` (`ok`, `below_target`, `incomplete`); food cost, gross margin, food cost % and GP% stay visible.
   - **Chef (V6.2):** `plate_margins_pct()` returns `recipe_key, food_cost_pct, gp_pct, status` with status ∈ `ok`, `below_target`, `incomplete` only (no fixed-cost state, no € column).
7. **Fixed cost (PRD 1.4 default basis, AT6).**
   - `fixed_cost_period` holds monthly totals `rent`, `labour`, `utilities` (€, entered net of VAT, Q5) per `month` (first day). Append-only: a correction is a new row for the same month; the newest row of a month wins. The figures **in force on date D** are those of the greatest `month ≤ month(D)` (carry forward until a new month is entered, Q5).
   - Plates sold: `app.plates_sold(p_tenant, p_from date, p_to date) returns numeric` (half portions count 0.5 from M19). **M8's body returns 0** (no sales exist before M19); M19/M21 replace the body with `create or replace` (same signature). Window for date D: business dates `D − 30` to `D − 1` (the 30 complete days before D).
   - `fixed_cost_per_plate(D)` = monthly total ÷ plates sold; **null when plates sold is 0** (`reason` `no_sales`) **or no period is in force** (`reason` `no_fixed_costs`). Food cost % and GP% never depend on it.
   - **Testing before sales exist:** pgTAP replaces `app.plates_sold` inside the test transaction (as M6's `046` stubs `alert_rules_body`) with a body returning 1 500, proving the division; the same file proves the real M8 body returns 0. The function is not executable by any API role.
   - Who reads and enters: **owner only** (founder 2026-10-23: fixed costs owner only; replaces §3.11.2 `fixed_cost_period` RW owner/admin). Screen `/costi/impostazioni` (the fixed-cost form is shown to the owner only).
8. **Tenant cost settings live in `tenant.settings` and hold no € value.** Keys `avg_cost_n` (integer 1–10, default 3), `price_variance_pct` (1–100, 2 decimals, default 10), `sale_vat_pct` (0–30, 2 decimals, default 10). Every member reads the tenant row, so **no € figure may ever go into `settings`** (fixed costs have their own owner-only table). Defaults live in one SQL function, `app.cost_settings(p_tenant)`; writes only through `public.update_cost_settings` (owner/admin, strict keys and ranges, `audit_event` `tenant.settings_changed` with the changed keys). These settings have no history: changing N or the VAT rate changes theoretical past costs, never frozen ones (ADR-008, accepted).
9. **Price variance inside `approve_invoice` (PRD 1.4, AT5) — and an alert never undoes an approval.**
   - **Which change:** for each ingredient with at least one price row in this invoice, the line with the largest absolute change is compared with the **baseline** = moving average of the last N purchases of that ingredient **in force on the invoice date, excluding this invoice's rows** (the "moving average" of PRD 1.4, whatever the ingredient's basis setting). `change_pct = (new − baseline) ÷ baseline × 100`. Alert when `abs(change_pct) > price_variance_pct` (strictly more; increases and decreases, Q9). No baseline (first purchase) or a baseline of 0 → no alert.
   - **One alert per ingredient per invoice**, `kind 'price_variance'`, `entity_type 'price_history'`, `entity_id` = the chosen price row's id. The M6 table proposed `ingredient` as the entity; with the dedup key `(kind, entity, business_date)` a second invoice moving the same ingredient on the same day would then be silently dropped. A price row is approved exactly once, so this key never collides, and the payload carries `ingredient_id`. Business date = the approval moment's Rome date (`app.alert_business_date`, default). Severity `warning`. Audience `{owner}`; `cost_bearing` true (the M6 check constraint forbids the chef).
   - **Affected recipes:** every **active** recipe whose current version reaches the ingredient, walking **upwards** through sub-recipe and batch lines (distinct-node walk, depth ≤ 13), so a beef price reaches the stew batch, the plate using the stew's portions and any plate using a sub that contains beef. Their **new** costs are computed after this approval's price rows are written (same transaction), at `p_at = null` (current), with the ingredient's own basis.
   - **Payload (M6 conventions, `_eur` money keys, owner-only text allowed):** `{ingredient_id, ingredient_name, unit, invoice_id, invoice_number, supplier_id, supplier_name, new_price_eur, baseline_eur, change_pct, threshold_pct, purchases_in_baseline, recipes_count, recipes_listed, recipes: [{recipe_key, type, name, unit_cost_eur, cost_unit, margin_eur?, gp_pct?, target_gp_pct?, status?}]}`. Names are cut to 80 characters. Plates carry the new `margin_eur` (gross margin), `gp_pct`, `target_gp_pct`, `status`; batches and subs carry their new unit cost.
   - **Size bound (8 KB cap of `alert_payload_ck`):** at most `PRICE_ALERT_MAX_RECIPES` = 20 entries, ordered plates first (status `below_target` and `incomplete` first, then `gp_pct` ascending), then batches, then subs, each by name; `recipes_count` is the full count and `recipes_listed` the entries kept. After building, while `octet_length(payload::text) > 7680` the last entry is dropped (a hard guarantee whatever the name lengths). The renderer says "e altre N ricette". Chosen over a summary-only alert because the owner needs the worst plates named; chosen over several alerts per ingredient because AT5 asks for one alert.
   - **`app.try_raise_alert` (M7 §8):** same parameters as `app.raise_alert` plus `p_caller text`; returns the alert id, or null when refused. It calls `raise_alert` inside a nested `begin … exception when sqlstate 'P0001'` block (a savepoint). If the message is one of the guard codes (`app.alert_guard_codes()`: `unknown_alert_kind`, `alert_audience_invalid`, `alert_audience_cost`, `alert_payload_cost_key`, `alert_payload_invalid`, `alert_payload_cost_value`), the block's work is already rolled back; the handler (which runs in the outer transaction) inserts one row into **`app.alert_refusal`** `(tenant_id, kind, entity_type, entity_id, caller, code, sqlstate, at)` — no payload, no message text — does `raise warning 'alert refused: % %', kind, code` and returns null. Any other error (P0002 `tenant_not_found`, a bug) is re-raised with `raise;` and is not swallowed by `try_raise_alert`.
   - **The variance check as a whole is contained too.** `approve_invoice` calls `app.price_variance_check(tenant, invoice)` inside its own `begin … exception when others` block after every ledger write. If the check fails for any reason other than a refused alert (which `try_raise_alert` already absorbed), the block rolls back to its savepoint, one `app.alert_refusal` row is written with `code 'check_failed'` and the SQLSTATE, a warning is raised, and **the approval commits**. Rationale: the approval is the ledger write the owner asked for; the alert is advice. A bug in the cost engine must not stop invoices being booked; it is recorded where M13's job-health check can read it (§6). `query_canceled` is not caught by `others` (Postgres), so a statement timeout still aborts the whole approval.
   - **Concurrency (two approvals at once).** Two approvals of different invoices that share an ingredient must see each other's price rows, or both would compare with the same old average. After the `supplier_item` locks, `approve_invoice` takes one **transaction advisory lock per ingredient of the invoice, in ingredient id order** (`app.lock_ingredient_costs`, two-integer form: class `hashtext('ingredient_cost')`, object `hashtext(tenant_id::text || ingredient_id::text)`; a hash collision only adds a wait). The second approval waits, then (READ COMMITTED, new snapshot per statement) its baseline includes the first's rows. Race case `variance-twice` (step 10).
   - **Concurrency with recipe edits.** Before the variance check, `approve_invoice` takes the recipe graph lock **in shared mode** (`pg_advisory_xact_lock_shared` on the same key as M7's exclusive `app.lock_recipe_graph`). Approvals do not block each other; a recipe version in progress makes the approval wait and vice versa, so the affected-recipe list and the new margins come from one stable graph. Race cases `approve-vs-version` and `version-after-approve` (step 10).
   - **Full lock order of `approve_invoice` after M8:** invoice row (`for no key update`) → duplicate advisory lock → `supplier_item` rows by id (`for update`) → ingredient cost advisory locks by ingredient id → inserts and updates of M5 → recipe graph lock (shared) → variance check. Recipe writers take only the graph lock (exclusive) and then `recipe_key` rows; they never wait for an invoice, item or ingredient cost lock, so no wait cycle exists. `set_ingredient_cost_basis` takes the ingredient cost lock alone. Both locks are taken **outside** the contained block, so a rolled-back check cannot release them early.
   - **M5 untouched in behaviour:** same signature, same gates, same return value `{payableId, amount, dueDate, receipts}` (so `035` and the review screen need no change); the variance check runs after the `audit_event` insert, i.e. after the M5 plan's "step 6" and after every other M5 write.
10. **V6 in Postgres (founder 2026-10-23).**
    - New cost tables: `ingredient_cost_basis` has RLS SELECT for owner/admin only, `fixed_cost_period` for the owner only; existing cost tables (`price_history`, `stock_movement`, `supplier_item`, `payable`, `invoice_line`) already exclude the chef (M4/M5).
    - Every public € function (`ingredient_costs`, `recipe_cost_at`, `recipe_costs`, `plate_costs`, `fixed_cost_status`, `cost_settings`) calls `app.require_cost_reader()` first: owner/admin, else 42501; `fixed_cost_status` calls `app.require_fixed_cost_reader()` instead (owner, else 42501), and `plate_costs` nulls the fixed-cost fields for any non-owner. The view `v_recipe_cost` selects from `public.recipe_costs()`, so the chef and waiter get 42501 on it.
    - The chef's only cost-derived read is `public.plate_margins_pct()` (definer, owner/admin/chef, else 42501); its result type has exactly four columns, no € column.
    - The engine (`app.recipe_costs`, `app.ingredient_unit_costs`, `app.plate_cost_rows`, `app.plates_sold`, `app.fixed_cost_at`) is in schema `app`, not executable by any API role.
    - A pgTAP catalogue scan (step 5) checks, as chef A on a fixture with a row in every table: every public relation the chef can read has no column matching the cost pattern except the M7 allow-list (`recipe_price.sale_price`, `recipe_price.target_gp_pct`), and every public function whose name matches `(cost|margin|price)` other than `plate_margins_pct` raises 42501 for the chef. A JSON key allow-list scan (steps 12 and 15) covers the API.
11. **Role matrix in brief:** owner and admin read and write every M8 object except fixed costs, which are owner only for read and write (founder 2026-10-23: fixed costs owner only); the chef reads `plate_margins_pct()` only; the waiter reads nothing. Changing the basis and settings, and running the cost check: owner and admin (Q10, Q11); entering fixed costs: owner only.
12. **Cost check for Gate 1 (AT9).** `POST /api/admin/cost-check` compares the chef's food cost per plate with the app's current food cost. Pure TS over the `plate_costs()` result (no new SQL, nothing stored): rows matched by normalised name (the M7 `name_norm` rule) to active plates; `diff_pct = (app − sheet) ÷ sheet × 100`, listed when `abs(diff_pct) > COST_CHECK_TOLERANCE_PCT` (5; "within 5%" passes, so exactly 5 % is not listed); unmatched names, duplicate names, incomplete plates and sheet values ≤ 0 are reported separately, never guessed. Owner and admin only (the admin is DigiAlto support running Gate 1).
13. **Contract for later milestones** (§3.8): the engine functions are `stable`, take an explicit tenant and instant, never write, and return incomplete instead of 0.

### 3.4 Flows

1. **Approval with a price change:** `POST /api/invoices/:id/approve` (unchanged route) → `approve_invoice` → ingredient cost locks → M5 writes → graph lock (shared) → `price_variance_check` → per ingredient over the threshold: payload → `try_raise_alert` → one `alert` row (owner) or one `alert_refusal` row → commit. The owner sees it on `/avvisi` (M6 screen, new template).
2. **Owner cost screen:** `/costi` → `GET /api/costs/plates?at=` → `plate_costs(p_date)` + `fixed_cost_status(p_date)` → list. A plate → `/costi/ricette/[key]` → `GET /api/recipes/:key/cost?at=` → `recipe_cost_at`.
3. **Chef margins:** `/margini` → `GET /api/costs/plates/percentages` → `plate_margins_pct()` + names and `target_gp_pct` from `v_recipe_current` and `recipe_price` (chef RLS) → list.
4. **Basis switch:** `/costi/ingredienti` → "Usa ultimo prezzo" → `PUT /api/ingredients/:id/cost-basis {basis: 'last_price'}` → `set_ingredient_cost_basis` → the next read of every cost uses it.
5. **Fixed costs and settings:** `/costi/impostazioni` → `POST /api/fixed-costs` → `enter_fixed_costs`; `PATCH /api/settings` → `update_cost_settings`.
6. **Gate 1 check:** `/costi/verifica` → CSV pasted or chosen → `POST /api/admin/cost-check` → TS parse → `plate_costs()` → compare → result (nothing stored).

### 3.8 Contract for M14, M17, M19, M22 (cost freezing)

- **The engine never writes.** `app.ingredient_unit_costs`, `app.recipe_costs`, `app.recipe_cost_lines`, `app.plate_cost_rows` are `stable`, take an explicit tenant and instant, and return null + `incomplete` instead of 0.
- **M14 `produce_batch`:** inside its transaction, value the confirmed actual inputs with `app.ingredient_unit_costs(tenant, ids, produced_at)` (the basis in force at production), store total and per-portion cost in `batch_cost`; `batch.recipe_version` pins the version. M14 values the actual inputs, not the recipe's theoretical lines, so M8 adds no version-cost function for it. An incomplete input is stored as an incomplete batch cost (M14 must add a flag column; it must not store 0). M14 test 5 ("a price change after production does not change `batch_cost`") holds because nothing recomputes the copy.
- **M19 `fire_course`:** per fired plate line, `order_line_cost.unit_cost_at_sale` = Σ batch portions depleted × that batch's frozen `batch_cost` per portion + direct ingredient and sub lines priced with the engine at the firing instant (`app.recipe_cost_lines(tenant, recipe_id, fired_at)` for those lines). An incomplete result is stored with a flag, not as 0. Firing never fails because of cost (plan §3.6 rule 8). Any alert raised there uses `app.try_raise_alert`.
- **M19/M21:** replace `app.plates_sold` (same signature) with Σ `qty` of fired, non-voided plate lines by business date; M8's `058` stub cases keep proving the division.
- **M22:** food cost % in KPIs uses the frozen `order_line_cost`, never the engine; the chef's KPI views follow V6 (percentages only).
- **M17:** a menu item's VAT rate (and its price, if it differs from `recipe_price.sale_price`) replaces `sale_vat_pct` in `app.plate_cost_rows` for mapped plates (plan change, §6).

## 4. Alternatives considered

- **Materialised cost table (or a stored moving average) refreshed inside `approve_invoice`:** rejected (ADR-008). A stored running value breaks the invariant, must be refreshed by every recipe version, basis switch and settings change, and cannot answer "as of" a date. Derived reads meet AT4 immediately.
- **Recipe cost in TypeScript (load the graph, compute in the server):** rejected. M14 and M19 must freeze costs inside their SQL transactions, and the price variance check runs inside `approve_invoice`; one SQL engine serves all three and the API.
- **A path-enumerating recursive CTE computing costs in one query (sum over paths):** rejected. It is what M7 round 2 removed (W^L rows on a diamond). The distinct-node walk plus bottom-up rounds is polynomial.
- **`ingredient.cost_basis` column (project plan §3.5.1):** rejected. The chef reads `ingredient` (V6 would need column-level care for a cost setting), and a mutable column cannot answer "basis in force on date D". An append-only owner/admin table does both.
- **Quantity-weighted moving average as the default:** rejected for the base plan (PRD says "over the last N purchases"); it is variant V1 behind Q1.
- **Price variance entity = ingredient (M6 proposal):** rejected; a second same-day approval would be dropped by dedup (§3.1.9).
- **One alert per invoice listing every ingredient:** rejected; AT5 asks for one alert per changed ingredient, and an invoice-wide list would hit the 8 KB cap sooner.
- **No baseline lock (accept nondeterministic baselines between concurrent approvals):** rejected; two +11 % deliveries approved together would both alert against the old average, or not, depending on timing. The per-ingredient lock costs nothing at 25 invoices a month.
- **Exclusive recipe graph lock in approvals:** rejected; it would serialise all approvals of a tenant. Shared mode orders approvals only against recipe edits.
- **Let a variance-check failure roll back the approval:** rejected (§3.1.9); a cost-engine bug would stop invoices being booked.
- **Catch every error inside `try_raise_alert`:** rejected; the M7 §8 contract catches only guard codes, so a bug in a ledger caller still surfaces where it is not wrapped.
- **Chef reads the € functions with JSON filtering:** rejected by the founder's decision (V6 must be enforced in Postgres).
- **Cost check in SQL with a staging table:** rejected; nothing needs storing, and the comparison is a pure function of the sheet and `plate_costs()`.

## 6. Risks and mitigations

- **Security / privacy**
  - *Definer functions bypass RLS:* every public M8 function checks the role first, uses an empty `search_path`, takes the tenant from `app.tenant_id()` and filters every query by it; the engine and `try_raise_alert` are in `app` with no API grant (steps 1–6, 8). Composite foreign keys keep basis rows on the tenant's ingredients (step 1).
  - *€ reaching the chef (Q16 = V6):* role checks in Postgres, a separate four-column chef function, a catalogue scan over every chef-readable relation and cost-named function, an API key allow-list and string scans (steps 4, 5, 12, 15, 17). Remaining, accepted by the founder: menu price × food cost % gives the chef a € figure.
  - *€ reaching the waiter:* no policy, no route, `004` extended (steps 1, 5).
  - *Price alerts:* cost-bearing, owner only by check constraint (M6); admin reads none (M6 decision).
  - *Personal data:* fixed costs are business totals, but a labour total of one or two employees can reveal pay. Owner only (founder 2026-10-23: fixed costs owner only), monthly totals only (hint on the form), no per-person field, retention follows project Q14. Alert payloads may name suppliers (possibly sole traders, project Q13), owner-only, as M6. `app.alert_refusal` holds ids and codes only. No new processor.
  - *Cost-check upload:* owner/admin, size and row caps, formula cells refused, nothing stored, errors carry row numbers and codes only (steps 11, 14).
- **Correctness**
  - *Changing `approve_invoice`:* `create or replace` with the M5 body unchanged and two additions; every M5 pgTAP file and race case runs unchanged (steps 8, 10). The lock order is extended only after the M5 locks (§3.1.9), and recipe writers never wait on approval locks, so no wait cycle is possible; `crossed-items` is re-run.
  - *Variance check failures hidden by containment:* each one leaves an `app.alert_refusal` row and a database warning. M13's job-health check should alert the owner when refusals appear (plan change below). Until M13, they are visible only in logs and the table.
  - *Historical theoretical costs move:* a late-approved invoice, a change of N or of the VAT setting changes `recipe_cost_at` for past dates; frozen costs (M14, M19) and reports built on them do not (ADR-008). The date field on `/costi` says "Costo teorico, con i dati registrati oggi." (step 16).
  - *Unpriced ingredients:* shown as incomplete, never 0; items never bought on an approved stock line stay incomplete (Q7).
  - *Rounding:* full precision internally, 4 dp for € and 2 dp for % in SQL outputs, cents on screen; tests compare SQL outputs at 4 dp.
  - *Many alerts from one invoice:* one alert per changed ingredient; an invoice with many changed ingredients produces as many alerts (bounded by its lines).
- **Unverified (checked in the named step):**
  - **T1** `pg_advisory_xact_lock_shared` and `pg_advisory_xact_lock` on the same two-integer key block each other as shared/exclusive (Postgres docs, advisory lock functions): proved by `approve-vs-version` and `version-after-approve` (step 10).
  - **T2** statements in a plpgsql exception handler run after the block's subtransaction is rolled back, and locks taken inside the block are released with it (Postgres docs, "Trapping Errors"): proved by `060` (marker row survives) and `061` (refusal row committed with the approval) (steps 6, 8). This is why the ingredient and graph locks are taken outside the contained block.
  - **T3** `raise;` inside a handler re-raises the original SQLSTATE: proved by `060` (P0002 propagates) (step 6).
  - **T4** the bottom-up rounds stay under 5 s on the 4 × 10 diamond plus 200 plates: `062` (step 9).
- **Performance / cost:** every cost read walks the graph; bounded by the depth cap, the distinct-node walk and `062`. No figure is promised. No provider, no running cost.
- **Dependencies:** none added.

## 7. Open questions for the founder

The questions below are open. **Their defaults are in force for this build**, and a different answer triggers the named variant.

- **Q1 Moving average: simple or weighted by quantity?** **Default in force:** the simple mean of the prices of the last N purchases (PRD wording) (→ V1).
- **Q2 Which "max price" do you want to see?** **Default in force:** the highest price of all recorded purchases up to the date (the same as "last 12 months" during the app's first year) (→ V7 for a rolling window).
- **Q3 VAT rate on sale prices.** Recipe prices are VAT included (M7 Q3). **Default in force:** one tenant rate `sale_vat_pct` = 10, editable by owner and admin; please confirm with the accountant. Dish-specific rates come with menu items (M17). If prices are entered without VAT (M7 Q3 → M7 V3), M8 follows V5.
- **Q4 GP% and the target: on food cost or after fixed costs?** **Default in force:** GP% = (price ex VAT − food cost) ÷ price ex VAT; "margine lordo" = price ex VAT − food cost; "margine netto" = price ex VAT − full cost; the target compares with GP% on food cost (→ V3).
- **Q5 Fixed costs: which figures, VAT, and months without an entry.** We need your monthly rent, labour and utilities (project Q4). **Default in force:** entered as monthly totals net of VAT; a month without an entry uses the last month entered. No real figure is loaded until you provide them. (Operational input, not a build variant.)
- **Q6 Fixed cost before 30 days of sales (project Q17).** **Default in force:** "not allocated" until sales exist (M19/M21); food cost % and GP% always shown (→ V4).
- **Q7 Ingredients never bought on an invoice** (cash purchases, samples). **Default in force:** cost unknown; every recipe using them shows "Costo incompleto" with the name (→ V2).
- **Q8 The chef's spreadsheet for Gate 1 (project Q2).** Which file and layout? **Default in force:** CSV `piatto;costo_cibo` (food cost per plate, VAT excluded, as our figures), tolerance ±5 % of the chef's figure, exactly 5 % counts as within (→ V6).
- **Q9 Price alerts: decreases too, and exactly at the threshold?** **Default in force:** increases and decreases both alert; only changes strictly above the threshold alert (10 % → 10,0 % does not, 10,1 % does); the comparison is with the moving average of the last N purchases before this invoice, whatever basis the ingredient uses (→ V8).
- **Q10 Who changes the calculation rules** (N, alert threshold, VAT) and fixed costs? **Default in force:** rules: owner and admin (DigiAlto support), every rule change recorded in the audit log (→ V9 for owner only). **Fixed costs: owner only, decided (founder 2026-10-23).**
- **Q11 Who switches an ingredient between average and last price?** **Default in force:** owner and admin; the chef cannot see or change it (V6) (→ V9 for owner only).
